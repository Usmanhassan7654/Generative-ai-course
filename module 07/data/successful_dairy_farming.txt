SUCCESSFUL DAIRY FARMING

A successful dairy farm begins with a clear plan. Decide how many animals the farm can feed, where milk will be sold, and how daily expenses will be recorded. Start with a manageable herd and expand only when feed, housing, labour, and a reliable buyer are available.

Choose healthy animals suited to the local climate and available feed. Keep identification and production records for each animal. A high purchase price or an attractive breed name does not guarantee a profitable animal. Compare milk output with the cost of feeding and caring for that animal.

Provide a balanced ration and a dependable supply of clean drinking water. Plan fodder production across seasons and preserve suitable surplus feed as silage or hay. Avoid sudden changes in feed. Ask a qualified local livestock professional to help match the ration to the animal's needs.

Comfortable housing supports consistent production. Provide shade, ventilation, dry resting areas, and drainage. In hot weather, reduce heat exposure and ensure animals can reach water easily. Keep feeding and walking areas clean and avoid overcrowding.

Clean milking routines protect milk quality. Wash hands, clean equipment, and use clean containers. Keep milk protected from dirt and cool it promptly using the facilities available. Follow the buyer's quality requirements and maintain a reliable collection schedule.

Work with a veterinarian on preventive health, vaccination, parasite control, and reproductive management. Observe animals every day and record changes in appetite, behaviour, and milk production. Separate newly purchased or sick animals as advised by the veterinarian. Follow prescribed medicine withdrawal periods before selling milk.

Record milk sold, feed purchased, labour, electricity, transport, and veterinary costs. Review these records every month. More milk does not automatically mean more profit if costs rise faster than income. Use farm records to identify waste and make small, measurable improvements.

Train workers, manage manure responsibly, and build a dependable relationship with buyers. Good dairy farming combines animal care, hygiene, feed planning, and careful business management. This text is general classroom material; farm-specific decisions need local professional advice.
